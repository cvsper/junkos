# Gatekeeper Playbook — when you can't reach the boss

*Supplement to the Aug 4 demand playbook. Written for Tracy, Aug 12 2026.*

The Aug 4 playbook assumed you'd reach the decision-maker. In practice most
dials land on a receptionist or personal assistant. That's not a failed call —
it's a different call with a different goal.

## The mindset shift

**Stop pitching the receptionist.** They can't say yes — they can only say no.
The moment you deliver the full pitch to a gatekeeper, the best possible
outcome is "I'll pass it along" (they usually won't, from memory).

A gatekeeper call is a WIN if you leave with any of these three things:
1. **A name** — who actually handles junk/cleanouts/disposal
2. **A direct route** — that person's email or cell
3. **A send** — your info pack physically in their inbox before you hang up

That's the whole game. Three questions, two minutes, next card.

## The gatekeeper opener

Don't ask for "the owner" or "the manager" — that's what every salesperson
says and it triggers the block. Ask the operational question instead:

> "Hi, this is Tracy with Umuve — quick question, maybe you can help me.
> When a tenant leaves furniture behind, or there's a pile that needs to
> disappear — who handles that for you guys?"

Why it works: receptionists deflect sales pitches all day, but they *answer
questions about how the office works* all day too. You're asking the second
kind. "Maybe you can help me" makes them the expert instead of the obstacle.

They'll usually answer one of three ways:

**"That would be [name]."** → Gold. "Perfect — is [name] in? ... No? No
problem, what's the best email for them? I'll send over a one-pager they can
look at in 30 seconds." → **Email it from the desk while you're still on the
line**, then: "Just sent it — it's from Umuve. When's [name] usually in, so I
can follow up?"

**"You can send us something."** → "Happy to — who should I address it to,
so it doesn't just sit in the general inbox?" (Get the name — 'info@' emails
die.) Then send it live on the call: "Sent! It's the one from Umuve — one
page, 30 seconds. I'll check back Thursday."

**"We're not interested."** → A receptionist's "not interested" is not the
company's no — they're clearing their line. "Totally understand — last thing:
if a unit ever needs a same-day cleanout, we're one text away. I'll leave
our info with you either way." Send the text, log **Voicemail** (not "not
interested" — the decision-maker never heard the pitch), move on.

## Send it WHILE you're on the phone

This is the biggest single upgrade. "I'll send you something" from a cold
caller means nothing — everyone says it and half never do. "I just sent it,
it should be in your inbox now — see it?" is a pattern interrupt. It proves
you're real, it makes them look, and it gives your follow-up call a hook.

The desk now does this: on every card there's a **"They said 'send us
something'?"** block — a Text-it button (their number, or swap in the cell
they give you) and an Email-it button. Both go out from the Umuve identity,
both are written so the gatekeeper can forward them straight to the boss.
The email you type is saved to the card, so it's there on the follow-up.

## The follow-up call (2–3 days later)

Now you're not cold anymore. You have a name and a thing to reference:

> "Hi, it's Tracy with Umuve — I sent over that one-pager for [name] on
> Tuesday. Did they get a chance to look? ... Could you check with them? I'll
> hold." (Or: "When's a good time to catch [name] directly?")

Asking *by name* about *a specific email* gets treated like ongoing business,
not a cold call. Some receptionists will just put you through.

## Outcome logging cheat sheet

- Talked only to a gatekeeper, sent the info → **Sent the link** (2-day follow-up)
- Gatekeeper took a message, nothing sent → **Voicemail** (3-day retry)
- Gatekeeper gave a flat no *from the decision-maker* ("Mr. K saw it and
  said no") → **Not interested** (dead)
- Got the boss's cell → paste it in the Note, then text the info to that cell

## Expectations (so you don't beat yourself up)

B2B through gatekeepers converts on the *third to fifth* touch, not the
first. A day of "all receptionists, no signups" where you banked 20 names and
15 sends is a *good day* — those are next week's warm calls. Track sends and
names, not just YESes.
