What we haul

Anything that grew, and is already down.

We're the truck, not the saw. If it's cut, raked, pulled or blown down, we'll load it — loose, bagged, wet, doesn't matter. Two things stay out of the pile because they're priced differently, and one we don't handle at all.

In the pile

  • ✓Palm fronds, seed pods and coconuts — the South Florida staple
  • ✓Branches, limbs and trunk sections a two-person crew can lift
  • ✓Hedge and shrub trimmings, ficus and bougainvillea cuttings
  • ✓Bagged leaves, grass clippings and pulled weeds
  • ✓Storm debris: downed vegetation, blown-in fronds, snapped fence-line growth
  • ✓Dead plants, old sod rolls, potted-plant remains

  • –Soil, sand, rock and pavers. Heavy material goes as construction debris — keep it in its own pile.

After the storm, the curb becomes a waiting room.

Every summer the sequence is the same. Municipal debris crews are thorough, but they run routes across whole counties, and the pile in front of your house sits until they get to your street. That's the gap we fill.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

How much does yard waste removal cost in Palm Beach County?

$30 per cubic yard plus an 8% service fee, with a $119 minimum per pickup. A cubic yard is a 3 × 3 × 3 ft pile. Three cubic yards or less lands on the minimum; five cubic yards is $162 all-in; ten is $324. Same-day pickup adds 25%, next-day adds 10% and weekends add 15%. The price is fixed before the crew arrives.

How do I estimate how many cubic yards I have?

One cubic yard is a pile about 3 feet long, 3 feet wide and 3 feet tall. A standard pickup-truck bed, filled level, holds roughly 2 cubic yards; heaped, about 3. If you're unsure, text a photo to (844) 435-6005 and we'll count it for you and lock the number.

Do you pick up storm debris after a hurricane?

Yes — that's most of our yard-waste work between June and November. After a storm, municipal debris crews work through their routes over days or weeks, and the pile stays at your curb until they reach you. We haul it on the day you book, priced by the cubic yard, so you're not waiting on a county schedule.

Do you cut down trees or trim palms?

No. We haul what's already down: fronds, branches, trunks that have been cut into sections a crew can lift, hedge trimmings, bagged leaves and clippings. If you need a tree trimmed or removed, book an arborist first and us second — or ask your tree crew what they'll leave behind and we'll price the pile.

Does the pile need to be bagged or bundled?

No. City curbside pickup usually has bagging and bundle-length rules; we don't. Loose piles are fine. If you do bag leaves or clippings, that's fine too — we take bags as-is. Please keep soil, rocks and construction debris out of the pile; those are priced separately.

What happens to the yard waste after you take it?

Vegetation goes to a licensed facility that accepts yard waste separately from household trash. Where local facilities offer it, that means mulching or composting rather than landfill. Bags and any non-plant material in the pile are separated out at the transfer station.
