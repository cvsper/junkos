On the day

Usually under half an hour on site.

  1. 1ARRIVE

    We text before we knock

    You get a 30-minute heads-up. The appliance is already unplugged, emptied and — if it's a freezer — defrosted.

  2. 2DISCONNECT

    Hoses off, doors secured

    Washer hoses come off at the valve, dryer vents unclamp, fridge doors get strapped shut so nothing swings on the stairs.

  3. 3CARRY

    Dolly, straps, out the door

    Two people, an appliance dolly, floor protection where it's tile or wood. Through the garage or the front door, whichever is straighter.

  4. 4HAUL

    To a facility that handles refrigerant

    Fridges, freezers and AC units go where certified technicians recover the refrigerant before the unit is scrapped, as federal rules require. Nothing gets vented.

  5. 5SCRAP

    The metal gets a second life

    Steel cabinets, copper coils and motors are the most recyclable junk there is. They're separated for scrap wherever the facility accepts them.

Before we arrive

Four things that make it go fast.

The day before

Empty and defrost. Freezers and fridge freezers need 24 hours unplugged with the door open and a towel down. A frozen unit is heavier and leaks in the truck.

Connections

Unplug it. Shut washer valves. Gas or hard-wired 220V? Book the plumber or electrician first — we can't touch those.

The path

Clear the route. Rugs up, doors propped, cars out of the garage. Measure the narrowest doorway if the fridge is a wide French-door model.

Tell us

Stairs, elevators, condo rules. Flights above ground are $15 each; some buildings want a service elevator booked. Put it in the notes.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

How much does it cost to remove a refrigerator in Palm Beach County?

A full-size refrigerator is $149 all-in with Umuve anywhere in Palm Beach, Broward or Miami-Dade: a $129 list price, a $10 appliance recycling fee for the refrigerant handling, and our 8% service fee. Same-day pickup adds 25%, next-day 10% and weekends 15%. Any single small appliance — microwave, bar fridge, stove — books at our $119 minimum.

Do I need to disconnect the appliance before you arrive?

Plug-in appliances: just unplug them. Washers: turn the water valves off and we'll pull the hoses. Gas dryers and gas stoves must have the gas shut off and the line capped by a licensed plumber or gas fitter before we arrive — our crews don't cap gas lines. Hard-wired 220V units and built-in dishwashers or wall ovens need to be disconnected by an electrician or plumber first; once it's freestanding, we take it.

What happens to the refrigerant in my old fridge or freezer?

Federal law (EPA Section 608) requires refrigerant to be recovered by certified technicians before a refrigerator, freezer or AC unit is scrapped. Your appliance goes to a licensed transfer station or scrap facility that does that recovery; we never vent it. The $10 appliance recycling fee on those items covers that handling.

Can you take a dishwasher or wall oven that's still installed?

Once the water, drain and power are disconnected and the unit is pulled free of the cabinet, yes. Our crews carry appliances out; they don't work under sinks or inside electrical panels, so a plumber or electrician handles the disconnect on built-ins. If the unit is already freestanding, book it like any other appliance.

Is it cheaper to have several appliances picked up at once?

Yes. Four to seven items on one booking get 10% off the item prices, eight to fifteen get 15% off, and sixteen or more get 20% off. A fridge, washer, dryer and dishwasher together come to $425 all-in instead of four separate trips.

Do you take appliances from apartments and condos with stairs or elevators?

Yes. Ground level and elevator buildings are included in the price. Flights of stairs above ground level are $15 per flight, added at booking so the number is still locked before we arrive. Tell us about narrow doorways, tight turns or loading-dock rules when you book.
