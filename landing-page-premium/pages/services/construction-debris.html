What goes in the truck

Everything a remodel leaves behind — with two exceptions.

Renovation debris goes to a construction-and-demolition facility, not the household trash stream, and those facilities charge by the ton. Our per-yard price already includes the tipping fees. What we can't take is the stuff no junk truck should touch.

We take

  • ✓Drywall, plaster and lath — bagged or in sheets
  • ✓Tile, thinset, backer board and mortar bags
  • ✓Cabinets, vanities, countertops (laminate, wood, stone)
  • ✓Lumber, plywood, trim, doors and framing offcuts
  • ✓Carpet, padding, vinyl plank and laminate flooring
  • ✓Tubs, toilets, sinks, fixtures and old windows
  • ✓Roofing shingles, fascia, gutters and metal
  • ✓Fencing, pavers and small amounts of concrete

We don't take

  • –Anything that may contain asbestos. Popcorn ceilings, 9×9 floor tile and mastic, some pipe insulation and older siding need testing and a licensed abatement contractor — not a junk truck. If the house is older and you're not sure, test first.
  • –Wet paint, solvents, fuel and chemicals. Palm Beach County's household hazardous waste program takes them free from residents.
  • –Loads that are mostly concrete or masonry. We can often do it, but it's quoted from a photo — see the heavy-debris note below.

Not sure which side something is on? Text a photo to (844) 435-6005 and you'll have an answer in minutes.

Counting yards

A cubic yard is a three-foot cube. Here's what that looks like.

Nobody has a tape measure out at the end of a demo day, so use the pile. These are rough guides for typical South Florida remodels — a photo gets you an exact count before you book.

  1. 1yard

    A 3 × 3 × 3 ft cube — 27 cubic feet. About what fits in six or seven contractor bags of drywall, or a stack of broken tile the size of a washing machine.

  2. 2–2.5yards

    A level load in a full-size pickup bed. Most people's mental unit. Two of these and you're at the five-yard mark.

  3. 3–5yards

    A bathroom gut — wall and floor tile, tub or shower surround, vanity, toilet, the drywall behind it. Under five yards is the $45 list rate all the way.

  4. 6–10yards

    A kitchen demo — cabinets, countertops, flooring, a wall of drywall. Beyond five yards each additional yard is $60: that's the rate real C&D disposal costs at this scale, and it's the same curve our commercial loads follow.

  5. 10+yards

    Whole-house or multi-room jobs. Price it here or set up a contractor account with per-load rates at goumuve.com/partners.

On the day

Four things that make it go fast.

Where it sits

Driveway, garage or side yard. A pile we can back up to loads in a fraction of the time. Inside the house is fine too — just tell us which floor.

Bag what you can

Drywall in contractor bags, tile in buckets. Not required — the price is by volume either way — but fewer trips to the truck means we're gone sooner.

Pull the nails

Or tell us you didn't. Lumber with nails and screws still loads; we just need to know so nobody grabs it barehanded.

Keep it separate

Debris in one pile, hazards in another. Paint cans, solvents and anything suspected of asbestos stay out of the pile — see the list above.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

How much does construction debris removal cost in Palm Beach County?

We price renovation debris by volume: $45 per cubic yard for the first five yards and $60 per yard beyond that, plus an 8% service fee, with a $119 job minimum. A 4-yard bathroom demo comes to $194.40 all-in; a 10-yard kitchen demo comes to $567. Same-day pickup adds 25%, next-day 10%, weekends 15%.

How big is a cubic yard of debris?

A cubic yard is a 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft cube — 27 cubic feet. A level load in a full-size pickup bed is roughly 2 to 2.5 cubic yards. If you can text us a photo of the pile, we'll count the yards for you before you book.

Do I need to bag or stack the debris?

No, but it helps. Piles we can reach from a driveway, garage or side yard load fastest. Drywall in contractor bags, tile in buckets, and cabinets stacked flat all mean fewer trips to the truck — the price is by volume either way.

What construction debris can't you take?

Anything that may contain asbestos — popcorn ceilings, floor tile and mastic from older homes, some pipe insulation — needs testing and a licensed abatement contractor, not a junk truck. We also don't take wet paint, solvents, fuel or other hazardous chemicals. Drywall, tile, wood, cabinets, fixtures, carpet, roofing and metal are all fine.

Is heavy debris like tile and concrete priced differently?

We price by volume, not weight. Tile, concrete and roofing are dense, though, and disposal facilities charge by the ton — so a load that is mostly masonry may need to be split across trips or quoted from a photo first. Text one to (844) 435-6005 and you'll have an exact number.

Do you work with contractors on an ongoing basis?

Yes. Contractors, property managers and remodelers can set up a standing account with per-load commercial rates and one number for every job site. See goumuve.com/partners or call (561) 944-1636.
